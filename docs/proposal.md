## How to structure a video game design proposal

A good video game design proposal (often called a Game Design Document or GDD) explains what the game is, how it works, what makes it interesting, and how it can realistically be developed.

The key is to establish the game's identity and core gameplay before getting into individual mechanics, content or technical implementation.

### I'd structure the proposal around these 10 areas:

1. Game concept and vision
   What are you making, and why should someone play it?

- Describe the game in 2–3 sentences.
- Establish its genre, setting, theme and overall premise.
- Identify the central selling point: what makes it interesting or different?
- Define the intended player experience (relaxing, challenging, strategic, immersive, etc.).
  Approach: Write a short pitch that someone unfamiliar with the project can understand immediately.

2. Target audience and platform
   Who is the game for, and where will they play it?

- Define the intended audience and their gaming preferences.
- Choose platforms: PC, console, mobile, etc.
- Consider input methods (keyboard/mouse, controller, touchscreen).
- Establish whether the game is single-player, multiplayer or cooperative.
- Consider accessibility and expected session length.
  Approach: Start with your primary audience and platform. Avoid designing for everyone.

3. Core gameplay loop
   What does the player actually do, repeatedly?

- Identify the main actions available to the player.
- Define the sequence of actions that forms the gameplay loop.
- Explain what motivates players to repeat that loop.
- Identify short-, medium- and long-term rewards.
  Approach: Describe a typical 5–10 minute gameplay session. If the loop isn't engaging, additional content won't fix it.

4. Game mechanics and systems
   What rules govern the game world?

- Define movement, interaction, combat, trading, crafting, exploration, etc., as applicable.
- Establish how resources, progression and rewards work.
- Explain relationships between different mechanics.
- Identify important constraints and rules.
  Approach: Prioritise mechanics that directly support the core gameplay loop. Define their inputs, outputs and interactions.

5. World, setting and narrative
   Where does the game take place, and what is happening?

- Establish the setting, atmosphere and world-building.
- Define the story, if applicable.
- Identify important characters, factions and locations.
- Determine how much narrative is delivered through dialogue, gameplay and environmental storytelling.
  Approach: Build enough world and narrative to support the gameplay. Avoid creating extensive lore that has no impact on the player experience.

6. Progression and player motivation
   Why does the player keep playing?

- Define character, equipment, technology or economic progression.
- Establish unlockable content and milestones.
- Consider difficulty curves and balancing.
- Define how players are rewarded for achievements and exploration.
- Consider replayability and endgame content.
  Approach: Map progression from the first 10 minutes through to the endgame. Make sure new abilities and rewards create meaningful gameplay opportunities.

7. Art direction and audio
   What should the game look and sound like?

- Establish the visual style (pixel art, realistic 3D, stylised 2D, etc.).
- Define colour palettes, environments, characters and UI aesthetics.
- Establish the musical direction and sound effects.
- Consider animation complexity and asset requirements.
  Approach: Create a small visual reference board with examples. Define a consistent artistic direction before producing large quantities of assets.

8. User interface and user experience
   How does the player interact with the game?

- Define menus, HUD elements, inventories, maps and information displays.
- Consider onboarding, tutorials and player feedback.
- Establish how information is presented without overwhelming the player.
- Consider accessibility and control customisation.
  Approach: Sketch the most important screens and gameplay HUD. Prioritise clarity and usability over visual complexity.

9. Technical design and development scope
   How will the game actually be built?

- Choose the game engine and supporting tools.
- Establish the target hardware and performance expectations.
- Identify technical requirements and dependencies.
- Estimate the complexity of mechanics, AI, physics, networking and asset production.
- Define what can realistically be developed.
  Approach: Separate essential features from optional features. Build a technical prototype of the riskiest mechanic before committing to full development.

10. Production plan and milestones
    How will the project progress from concept to finished game?

- Define development phases: prototype, vertical slice, production, testing and release.
- Establish milestones and deliverables.
- Estimate development time and resource requirements.
- Identify risks and dependencies.
- Define the minimum viable game (MVP).
  Approach: Work backwards from the smallest playable version of the game. Expand only after the core experience has been validated.

### Recommended structure of the actual document

I. Vision 1. Concept, 2. Audience, 5. World and narrative, 7. Art direction
II. Game design 3. Core loop, 4. Mechanics, 6. Progression, 8. UI/UX
III. Execution 9. Technical design, 10. Production plan

### A few important principles

1. Design gameplay before content. Establish what makes the game fun before designing hundreds of missions, locations or items.
2. Keep scope realistic. A small, polished game is better than an ambitious project that never gets finished.
3. Document decisions, not just ideas. Explain how mechanics work, not simply that they exist.
4. Use visual references. Diagrams, flowcharts, mock-ups and concept art often communicate ideas better than paragraphs.
5. Allow the document to evolve. A GDD is a living document, not a specification that must be perfect before development starts.
   My recommendation: Start with a relatively short 5–10 page proposal establishing the game's identity, gameplay loop and scope. Expand it into detailed mechanical and technical documentation as development progresses.
